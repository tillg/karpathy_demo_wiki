# Surface (topology) and the classification of surfaces (Wikipedia)

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_(topology) (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

A **surface** is a two-dimensional manifold: a topological space in which every point has an open neighbourhood
homeomorphic to an open piece of the plane. Up close, an ant on it sees a flat sheet.

**Orientability.** A surface is orientable if it has a consistent notion of "clockwise" everywhere — informally,
two sides. The **Möbius strip** is the standard example of a non-orientable surface: walk once along it and you come
back mirror-reversed. The **Klein bottle** is a closed non-orientable surface that cannot sit inside ordinary 3D
space without passing through itself.

**Classification theorem.** Every connected closed (compact, no boundary) surface is homeomorphic to exactly one of:

1. the sphere;
2. a connected sum of g tori (g ≥ 1) — a "doughnut with g holes";
3. a connected sum of k real projective planes (k ≥ 1).

Families 1 and 2 are orientable, family 3 is not. Two numbers settle which surface you have: its **Euler
characteristic** and whether it is orientable. For the orientable family χ = 2 − 2g, where g is the genus (number of
handles); for the non-orientable family χ = 2 − k.

**History.** The classification goes back to the 1860s; today there are several proofs — topological,
combinatorial and geometric.

Personal note: this is the first complete "list of all shapes" I've met in topology — every closed surface, without
exception, is on it.
