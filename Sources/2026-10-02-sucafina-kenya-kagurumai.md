# Kagurumai AA, Kenya — green coffee offering (Sucafina)

*Source: https://sucafina.com/na/offerings/kagurumai-aa (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

A green-coffee importer's profile of a washed AA lot from a cooperative washing station in Murang'a, Central Kenya.
It works as a compact example of how Kenyan coffee is grown and processed.

**Place.** Farms lie at about 1,500–1,630 m on fertile red volcanic soil. Cool nights and warm days slow the ripening
of the cherries so they build up more sugar.

**Farmers.** The Kagurumai Farmers Cooperative Society runs the washing station for 635 members. Each farms well under
a hectare with roughly 200–350 trees — typical of Kenya's smallholder, cooperative-based system.

**Varieties.** Four cultivars dominate: SL28 and SL34 (selections from the 1930s–40s with deep roots that make the
most of scarce water) and the newer Batian and Ruiru 11, bred for disease resistance and early bearing (fruit after
about two years).

**Harvest.** Ripe cherries are handpicked selectively in two seasons: an early crop around May–July and a late crop
around October–December.

**Processing.** Fully washed: after pulping, the coffee ferments for 16–24 hours, is washed, then soaked in clean water
for about 12 hours. The parchment dries on raised beds for 14–18 days, raked often and covered in the hottest part of
the afternoon and at night.

**Grade.** "AA" is a size grade: the largest beans, screen 17/18, above about 7.2 mm. It says nothing directly about
flavour.
