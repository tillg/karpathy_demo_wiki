# A history of topology (MacTutor)

*Source: https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/HistTopics/Topology_in_mathematics/ (accessed 2026-10-02).
Summarised in own words.*

MacTutor's short history traces topology from puzzles about shapes to a fully abstract theory.

- **Euler.** Between 1736 and 1752 Euler solved the Königsberg bridge problem and found the polyhedron formula
  v − e + f = 2 — a kind of geometry in which nothing is measured.
- **Lhuilier.** In 1813 he noticed that solids with holes break Euler's formula and need v − e + f = 2 − 2g, where g
  is the number of holes. MacTutor calls this the first known result about a topological invariant.
- **Listing** (1802–1882) introduced the word "topology" in 1847 and described the one-sided band in 1861 — four
  years before Möbius published his own description of what is now called the Möbius band.
- **Riemann** studied how surfaces are connected (1851, 1857), inventing Riemann surfaces to handle multi-valued
  functions from complex analysis.
- **Jordan** counted independent closed circuits on a surface and showed that their number is a topological
  invariant.
- **Betti** pushed the idea of connectivity to n dimensions (his definition later needed repair).
- **Poincaré** made the subject rigorous in his *Analysis situs* papers from 1895: homology, the fundamental group
  and homotopy, and a version of Euler's formula for higher-dimensional spaces.
- **Brouwer** around 1912 turned Poincaré's methods into a systematic theory.
- **Fréchet** (1906, metric spaces) and **Hausdorff** (1914, axioms for topological spaces) freed topology from
  distances entirely.

My reading: there is a slow shift from *counting* (vertices, holes, circuits) to *structure* — the 20th century
replaces numbers with groups and spaces with axioms.
