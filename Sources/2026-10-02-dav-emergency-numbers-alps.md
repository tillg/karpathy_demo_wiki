# DAV: Emergency call and rescue in the Alps

*Source: https://www.alpenverein.de/artikel/notruf-und-rettung-in-den-alpen_3802c636-0bd1-4f83-a33a-e9b266bd51d8 (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

A DAV reference page listing how to call for help in the Alpine countries.

**112.** The European emergency number is free in all EU member states, and mountaineers use it too. Without reception
on your own network, the page suggests switching the phone off and on and typing 112 instead of the PIN.

**Mountain-rescue numbers by country** (besides 112, these reach the mountain rescue more directly):

| Country | Number(s) given |
|---|---|
| Germany | 112 (mountain rescue is the Bergwacht) |
| Austria | 140 (alpine emergency, from within Austria) |
| Italy / South Tyrol | 112 |
| Switzerland | 1414 (Rega air rescue); 144 in the canton of Valais |
| France | 112; a separate number for the Chamonix rescue centre |
| Liechtenstein | 117 (police), 144 |
| Slovenia | 112 |

**Alpine distress signal.** When there is no phone: give an audible or visible signal six times within one minute,
pause one minute, repeat. The answer is three signals per minute.

**Apps.** The SOS-EU-ALP app (Bavaria, Austria, South Tyrol) and the Swiss Rega app, which sends the caller's
position to the rescue centre.

Numbers can change; check the current page before a trip.
