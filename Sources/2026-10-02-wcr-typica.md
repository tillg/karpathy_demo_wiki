# Typica — World Coffee Research variety catalogue

*Source: https://varieties.worldcoffeeresearch.org/varieties/typica (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

Typica traces back to south-western Ethiopia and travelled the world along colonial trade routes. A key step: in
1696 and 1699 seeds went from the Malabar coast of India to Batavia on Java, where the line now called Typica was
established. From there it reached the Americas, and it dominated plantations there until roughly the 1940s, when
it was gradually replaced because of its agronomic weaknesses. With Bourbon it forms the Bourbon–Typica group.

**Altitude.** The optimum shifts with latitude, from about 400–1,000 m beyond 15° latitude to above about 1,600 m
near the equator; it does best high up.

**Cup.** Quality potential at high altitude is rated very good, with good cup quality.

**Agronomy.** Tall plants with low yield; farms compensate by planting densely (about 3,000–4,000 plants per hectare
with single-stem pruning). It is highly susceptible to coffee leaf rust and susceptible to coffee berry disease and
nematodes.

Typica and Bourbon are the "parents" behind many varieties a home brewer sees on bags; knowing them helps read a
label.
