# DAV: Safe ski touring — 10 recommendations of the Deutscher Alpenverein

*Source: https://www.alpenverein.de/files/Die-10-DAV-Empfehlungen-zum-Skitourengehen_28216.pdf (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

A two-page leaflet ("Sicher Skitouren") with ten rules for ski, snowshoe and splitboard tours. It was agreed in June
2015 at the Pordoi Pass by the alpine clubs AVS, CAI, DAV, LAV, ÖAV, PZS and SAC. Basics come from courses;
experience grows step by step.

1. **Healthy and fit.** Ski touring is endurance sport; be honest about your fitness, avoid time pressure, pick a pace
   at which nobody in the group runs out of breath, and keep strength for the descent.
2. **Careful planning.** Use maps, guidebooks, the internet and experts for route, length, height gain and current
   conditions. Pay special attention to the weather: cold, wind and poor visibility sharply raise the accident risk.
   Plan alternatives and know the national mountain-rescue numbers (European emergency number 112).
3. **Complete kit.** Standard emergency kit is avalanche transceiver, probe and shovel, plus first-aid kit, bivvy bag
   and mobile phone. An airbag raises the chance of survival. Check the kit before starting and carry a repair kit.
4. **Avalanche bulletin.** Read it before the tour, with attention to danger level (1–5), where the danger spots are
   and what the main avalanche problem is.
5. **Weigh the risk.** People are poor at recognising avalanche danger, so base decisions on strategic risk methods
   ("reduction methods"), learn the warning signs in the terrain, avoid danger spots and turn back when in doubt.
6. **Breaks and orientation.** Drink, eat and rest to stay focused; hot isotonic drinks are recommended. Always know
   where you are and judge existing tracks critically.
7. **Keep distances.** About 10 m apart when climbing steep slopes, at least 30 m on the descent, and ski very steep
   slopes one at a time — to unload the snowpack and limit damage.
8. **Avoid falls.** Falls on the descent are the most common cause of ski-touring accidents. Good technique, adapted
   speed and a helmet help; frozen snow and rocky terrain bring a risk of a long fall.
9. **Small groups.** Up to six people; stay together, talk to other parties, tell someone your goal, route and return
   time. Solo tourers: small incidents can become serious emergencies.
10. **Respect nature.** Mind wildlife, respect protected areas, keep off young forest plantings, and share cars or use
    public transport.
