# Spring snow: where it lasts and when to go

*Clipped article (example.org).*

In spring the snowpack goes through a daily freeze–thaw cycle. After a clear, cold night the surface is frozen hard
in the morning and turns into **corn snow** once the sun softens the top few centimetres — the best skiing of the
year, but only for a few hours. Later in the day the snow gets wet and heavy, and wet-snow avalanches become likely.

Rules of thumb for an average winter in the Eastern Alps:

- **Altitude matters most.** Below about 1,500 m the snow is usually gone or patchy by late March. Between 1,500 and
  2,200 m south faces melt out in March, north faces often last into April. Above about 2,500 m, and on glaciers,
  touring usually works until mid-May.
- **Aspect is next.** North-facing slopes keep powder and hold snow weeks longer; south and east faces soften first
  and melt out first.
- **Start early.** Be on the summit around midday at the latest and off the steep slopes before the snow turns wet.

Every winter is different: check the snow depth and the avalanche bulletin before planning.
