# EAWS: European avalanche danger scale

*Source: https://www.avalanches.org/education/avalanche-danger-scale/ and https://www.avalanches.org/ (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

The European Avalanche Warning Services (EAWS) explain the common five-level danger scale used by European avalanche
warning services. EAWS describes its mission as supporting its
member services so that avalanche warnings prevent deaths and damage. Its site also offers a glossary, the avalanche
problems, standards for forecasters and fatality statistics.

The level depends on how stable the snowpack is, how often weak spots occur and how large avalanches may get, for a
given region and time.

| Level | Name | In short | Share of the winter / of fatalities (approx., as given) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Low | generally stable; triggering mostly by large loads in extreme terrain | — / ~5 % |
| 2 | Moderate | heightened danger on specific terrain features; mainly large loads | ~50 % of the season / ~30 % |
| 3 | Considerable | dangerous; triggering possible by small loads, remote triggering typical, especially with persistent weak layers | ~30 % of the season / ~50 % |
| 4 | High | very dangerous; triggering likely by small loads on many steep slopes; cracks and "whumpf" sounds typical | — / ~10 % |
| 5 | Very high | extraordinary; many very large natural avalanches even on moderate slopes; rarely forecast | — |

So level 3 accounts for about half of the deaths although it is forecast less often than level 2. The scale is a
summary; the bulletin text, danger spots and avalanche problems carry the detail.
