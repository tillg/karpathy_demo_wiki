# Poincaré conjecture (Wikipedia)

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poincar%C3%A9_conjecture (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

**The question.** In 1904 Henri Poincaré asked whether a closed three-dimensional manifold whose fundamental group
is trivial (every loop can be shrunk to a point) must be homeomorphic to the three-dimensional sphere. In other
words: is being simply connected enough to recognise the 3-sphere?

For surfaces the analogous statement is true and follows from the classification of surfaces — the sphere is the
only simply connected closed surface.

**Other dimensions first.** Oddly, the generalised question was settled in higher dimensions long before dimension
three: Stephen Smale proved it for dimensions five and up in 1961, and Michael Freedman did dimension four in 1982.

**Dimension three.** Grigori Perelman posted three papers on arXiv between November 2002 and July 2003. He built on
Richard S. Hamilton's programme of the **Ricci flow** — a way of letting the geometry of a manifold evolve so that it
smooths itself out, a bit like heat spreading — and added "Ricci flow with surgery" to cut out the singularities that
form along the way.

**Recognition.** *Science* named the proof its Breakthrough of the Year in 2006. Perelman was awarded the Fields
Medal in 2006 and the Clay Mathematics Institute's one-million-dollar Millennium Prize in 2010, and declined both,
saying Hamilton's contribution was no less than his own. The Poincaré conjecture is still the only one of the seven
Millennium Prize Problems that has been solved.
