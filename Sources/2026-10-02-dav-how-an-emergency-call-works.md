# DAV: "Wie funktioniert das? Notruf" (how an emergency call works)

*Source: https://www.alpenverein.de/artikel/wie-funktioniert-das-notruf_29889ad0-4363-4b8f-b52e-a5d0fecd7168 (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

A short DAV explainer, with an illustrated chain of steps, on calling for help in the mountains.

**Before the tour.** Carry a charged phone and, in winter, keep it warm close to the body. Write your own phone number
somewhere waterproof on or with the phone, so you can give it to the rescue centre. Know where you are using map and
altimeter, so the location can be described.

**Which number.** 112 gets priority on the network and works on a locked phone, but outside Germany and South Tyrol it
may first reach a general or police centre. National numbers such as 140 in Austria or 1414 (Rega) in Switzerland go
straight to mountain rescue but need normal network registration.

**During the call.** Stay calm and answer every question from the dispatcher — where it happened, what happened, how
many people are hurt and how badly, and the local conditions such as weather. Hang up only once the dispatcher ends the
call, then keep the line free for callbacks and avoid other calls.

**No reception.** Send two people to look for reception while the others give first aid. If that fails, use the
alpine distress signal: six signals per minute, then a pause; rescuers answer with three per minute.
