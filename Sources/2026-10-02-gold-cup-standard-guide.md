# Achieving the Gold Cup Standard (Seattle Coffee Gear)

*Source: https://www.seattlecoffeegear.com/blogs/learning-center/achieving-the-gold-cup-standard-your-guide-to-brewing-excellence (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

A practical explainer of the Gold Cup Standard, the brewing target popularised by the specialty coffee associations
for filter coffee. It defines a "sweet spot" with two numbers:

- **Strength — TDS 1.15–1.35 %.** Total dissolved solids: how much coffee is dissolved in the cup.
- **Extraction yield 18–22 %.** The share of the ground coffee's mass that ended up dissolved in the brew.

The two are independent: a cup can be strong but under-extracted, or weak but over-extracted. The standard is the
zone where both are in range, which most tasters find balanced.

**How to get there:**

- *Ratio:* about 55 g of coffee per litre of water, which is roughly 1:18. Weigh coffee and water on a scale instead
  of using scoops.
- *Water temperature:* about 195–205 °F (roughly 90–96 °C) where the water meets the grounds. Cooler water gives thin,
  sour cups; hotter water pushes towards bitterness.
- *Contact time:* about 4–8 minutes for automatic drip brewers, 3–4 minutes for pour-over, about 4 minutes of steeping
  for a French press.
- *Grind:* medium as the starting point for drip. Finer grounds slow the flow and extend contact time; coarser grounds
  do the opposite.
- *Water quality:* brewing water with about 75–150 ppm dissolved solids extracts well; for most home brewers filtered
  water is the safer choice.

**Troubleshooting.** Weak or flat cup: grind finer or use more coffee. Harsh or bitter cup: grind coarser. Change one
variable at a time — keep ratio and temperature fixed and tune with the grind.
