# Coffee production in Brazil (Wikipedia)

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_production_in_Brazil (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

Brazil has been the world's largest coffee producer for about 150 years and grows roughly a third of all coffee,
although its share has slipped since the 1950s as other countries expanded.

**History.** Coffee arrived in 1727, when Francisco de Melo Palheta planted it in Pará; legend has it he obtained the
seeds from French Guiana through diplomatic charm rather than official channels.

**Regions.** Plantations cover about 27,000 km², mostly in the south-east. Minas Gerais alone accounts for roughly
half of national output; São Paulo, Espírito Santo, Bahia, Rondônia and Paraná follow.

**Species.** About 70 percent of the crop is Arabica, concentrated in and around Minas Gerais, and about 30 percent
Robusta, mainly in Espírito Santo.

**Harvest and processing.** Most farms harvest in the dry season, June to September. Brazil relies almost entirely on
dry (natural) processing: whole cherries are cleaned and dried in the sun for about 8–10 days before the dried
fruit is hulled off and the beans are sorted. This is the main reason Brazilian coffees are associated with heavy
body and low acidity — the fruit dries on the seed.
