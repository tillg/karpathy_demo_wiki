# Coffee bean (Wikipedia)

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_bean (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

The "bean" is the seed of the coffee cherry. Two species matter commercially: *Coffea arabica* (Arabica) and
*Coffea canephora* (Robusta). Together they make up practically all coffee traded, at roughly 60 percent Arabica
and 40 percent Robusta.

**Arabica vs Robusta, as numbers:**

- Caffeine: Arabica about 0.8–1.4 %, Robusta about 1.7–4.0 % — Robusta carries roughly twice as much or more.
- Climate: Arabica prefers cooler conditions around 15–24 °C, Robusta warmer ones around 24–30 °C. That is why
  Arabica is the highland crop.
- Fat: Arabica beans contain noticeably more lipids (about 13.5–17.4 g per 100 g) than Robusta (about 9.8–10.7 g),
  part of why Arabica is credited with a more complex cup.

**Processing — getting the seed out of the fruit.** The article describes two classic routes:

- *Wet / washed:* the fruit flesh is removed mechanically, the seeds are left to ferment in water for about two
  days, and then washed to strip off the sticky remains before drying.
- *Dry / natural:* whole cherries are spread in the sun on concrete, brick or raised beds for two to three weeks and
  turned regularly so they dry evenly. It is the cheaper method and was long associated with lower grades.

A middle route (honey / pulped natural) is not described in detail here; see the honey-process guide.
