# Topology (Wikipedia)

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topology (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

Topology is the part of mathematics that studies the properties of a shape that survive continuous deformation:
stretching, bending and twisting are allowed, tearing and gluing are not. The abstract setting for this is the
**topological space** — a set with just enough structure to say which points are "near" each other, so that
continuous maps make sense.

**History in a few dates.**

- 1736 — Leonhard Euler solves the Seven Bridges of Königsberg problem, often named as the first result in the
  spirit of topology.
- 1750 — Euler's polyhedron formula V − E + F = 2, which some call the first theorem of topology.
- 1847 — Johann Benedict Listing coins the word "Topologie" in *Vorstudien zur Topologie*.
- 1895 — Henri Poincaré publishes *Analysis Situs*, introducing homotopy and homology ideas that founded algebraic
  topology.
- 1906 — Maurice Fréchet introduces metric spaces.
- 1914 — Felix Hausdorff coins the term "topological space".
- 1922 — Kazimierz Kuratowski gives a more general form of the definition.

**The coffee mug joke.** A topologist supposedly can't tell a coffee mug from a doughnut: a mug made of soft clay can
be reshaped into a ring torus without cutting or gluing, because both have exactly one hole (the handle).

**Main branches.**

- *General (point-set) topology* — the basic definitions: open sets, continuity, compactness, connectedness.
- *Algebraic topology* — attaches algebraic objects such as homotopy groups and homology to spaces in order to tell
  them apart.
- *Differential topology* — smooth manifolds and differentiable maps.
- *Geometric topology* — mainly manifolds in dimensions 2, 3 and 4, including knots.

My takeaway: topology throws away distances and angles and keeps only "how things hang together".
