# Geisha (Panama) — World Coffee Research variety catalogue

*Source: https://varieties.worldcoffeeresearch.org/varieties/geisha-panama (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

Geisha (often spelled Gesha) is not a bred variety but an Ethiopian landrace: seeds were collected from wild coffee
forests in Ethiopia in the 1930s, went to the Lyamungu research station in Tanzania, and from there in 1953 to CATIE
in Central America. It stayed obscure for decades until 2005, when a Panamanian farm entered it into the "Best of
Panama" competition. It sold for more than 20 US dollars a pound, an auction record at the time, and became the
emblem of high-end specialty coffee.

**Where it grows well.** Geisha needs altitude. The catalogue gives above about 1,600 m close to the equator and
above about 1,300 m in the 5–15° latitude band. Grown low, it loses much of what makes it special.

**Cup.** At high altitude the quality potential is rated exceptional. It is known for a delicate, perfumed cup:
floral and jasmine aromas with peach-like fruit.

**Agronomy — the catch.** Yield potential is low. Resistance to coffee leaf rust is only intermediate, and it is
susceptible to nematodes and coffee berry disease. That combination — low yield, demanding altitude, disease
risk — is part of why Geisha lots are expensive.
