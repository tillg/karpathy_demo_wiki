# DAV: Risk management in winter — making decisions in avalanche terrain

*Source: https://www.alpenverein.de/artikel/gute-entscheidungen-im-lawinengelaende-treffen_bb1fb2c1-2019-4533-b4c4-92f8ec2a3318 (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

An article by Markus Fleischmann and Lukas Fritz (DAV Panorama 2/2023, online March 2023) about how ski tourers can
reach sound decisions although avalanche risk can never be measured exactly.

**Risk formula.** The authors describe risk as hazard (how likely a release is) times consequences, minus the
measures taken — abbreviated in German as G × K − M = R.

**"Avalanche mantra".** A repeated five-step check used throughout the tour: probabilistic tools first, then a local
analysis of snow and terrain, then the possible consequences, then concrete measures, and finally the human factor.

**3x3 principle.** Decisions are organised over three phases (planning, on site, the single slope) and three factors
(terrain, conditions, people).

**Tools.** Probabilistic aids such as the SnowCard and the route-rating service skitourenguru.ch help choose terrain
to match the danger level. Slopes steeper than about 30 degrees count as avalanche terrain and deserve closer
analysis.

**Warning signs.** Fresh snow, wind slabs, shooting cracks, "whumpf" collapse sounds, recent avalanches and
suspicious layering in the snowpack.

**Human factor.** Biases, group dynamics, misperception, unclear communication and pressure to decide all distort
judgement; the group should name them openly.

The article is educational and does not replace training or the daily bulletin.
