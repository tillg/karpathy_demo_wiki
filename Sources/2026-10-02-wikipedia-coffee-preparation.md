# Coffee preparation (Wikipedia)

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_preparation (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

An overview of how ground coffee and hot water are brought together. Coffee is usually brewed with water close to
boiling. Recommended strengths vary by national standard, from about 55 g of coffee per litre of water (US) to about
63 g/L (Norway).

**Methods with concrete parameters:**

- *French press:* a tall, narrow cylinder with a plunger carrying a fine metal or nylon mesh. Coarse grounds steep for
  about 4–7 minutes before plunging. Leaving the coffee standing on the grounds after plunging over-extracts it and
  makes it bitter — pour it off.
- *Drip / pour-over:* hot water drips through a bed of grounds in a filter. The result is much less concentrated than
  espresso and contains less caffeine per cup.
- *AeroPress:* a manual device in which hand pressure pushes moderately hot water through the grounds in about 30
  seconds, into a single cup. Its taste sits between moka pot and French press.
- *Espresso:* water at about 91–95 °C is forced through finely ground coffee at between 8 and 18 bar, giving a small
  drink of about 30–60 ml.
- *Moka pot:* a three-chamber stovetop pot. Water boils in the bottom, and steam pressure of about one bar pushes it up
  through the coffee into the top chamber. The result is close to espresso strength but has no crema.

The article lists the Chemex among drip devices without giving specific parameters.
