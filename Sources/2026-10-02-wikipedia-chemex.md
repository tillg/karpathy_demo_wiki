# Chemex Coffeemaker (Wikipedia)

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemex_Coffeemaker (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

The Chemex is a pour-over brewer designed by Peter Schlumbohm in 1941. It is a single piece of glass in an
hourglass shape: a conical funnel on top flows into a flask below, so the brewer and the serving carafe are the same
object. Around the narrow waist sits a heat-proof wooden collar, turned and then split in two so it can be fitted
around the glass, and held in place by a leather tie — it serves as handle and insulation.

**Filters.** The Chemex uses its own bonded paper filters, which are clearly thicker and heavier than ordinary drip
filters. That is the core of its character: the dense paper holds back most of the coffee oils,
which gives a noticeably "clean", light-bodied cup. The thick filter is also reported to remove more cafestol, a
compound in coffee oil that can raise cholesterol.

**Design status.** In 1958 the Illinois Institute of Technology named it one of the best-designed products of modern
times, and it is part of the collection of the Museum of Modern Art in New York. It is one of the few kitchen objects
that is equally a design classic and an everyday brewing tool.

The article gives no brew recipe; for grind, ratio and time the vault falls back on the general pour-over guidance.
