# Coffee roasting (Wikipedia)

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_roasting (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

Roasting turns hard, grassy green beans into brown, aromatic coffee. Heat drives off water and starts browning
reactions — the Maillard reaction and caramelisation — that create most of coffee's flavour. Roasting also breaks
down part of the chlorogenic acids that make green coffee taste harsh.

**Roast levels, by bean temperature (approximate):**

- *Light* (cinnamon, "American"): about 196–205 °C, around the **first crack**, when the beans audibly pop and
  expand. Light body, high acidity, little obvious roast taste.
- *Medium* (city, full city): about 210–219 °C, after first crack. Origin character still present, roast notes
  starting to show.
- *Dark* (full city+, French, Italian): about 225–245 °C. The **second crack** comes around 224 °C. Full body,
  bittersweet roast flavours dominate.

**What changes with darker roasting:** acidity drops, roast and caramel/bitter notes grow, and the flavours that come
from variety, altitude and processing are increasingly masked. Light roasts are the way to taste an origin.

**Physical changes.** Beans lose about 15–18 % of their weight, mostly water, while their volume roughly doubles.

**Caffeine myth.** Dark roast is not "stronger" in caffeine. Because darker beans are lighter and less dense, a scoop
of light roast actually contains a bit more caffeine than the same scoop of dark roast.
