# Bourbon — World Coffee Research variety catalogue

*Source: https://varieties.worldcoffeeresearch.org/varieties/bourbon (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

Bourbon is one of the two foundational Arabica lines, the other being Typica; together they form the
"Bourbon–Typica" genetic group from which most traditional Latin American coffee descends. The catalogue calls it
one of the most culturally and genetically important Arabica varieties.

**History.** In the early 1700s French missionaries took coffee from Yemen to the island of Bourbon, today's La
Réunion, which gave the variety its name. From the mid-1800s it spread further, reaching Brazil around 1860 and then
South and Central America.

**Altitude.** Best at high altitude: above about 1,600 m near the equator, above about 1,300 m in the tropics and
above about 1,000 m in subtropical zones.

**Cup.** Quality potential at high altitude is rated very good, with excellent cup quality — a reference point for
sweet, classic Latin American coffee.

**Agronomy.** Medium yield, which is low by modern standards. It is susceptible to coffee leaf rust, coffee berry
disease and nematodes, which is why many farms replaced it with more resistant cultivars.
