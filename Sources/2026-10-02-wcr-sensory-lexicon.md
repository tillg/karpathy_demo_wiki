# Sensory Lexicon (World Coffee Research)

*Source: https://worldcoffeeresearch.org/resources/sensory-lexicon (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

World Coffee Research's Sensory Lexicon is a shared vocabulary for describing how coffee tastes, smells and feels in
the mouth. It was developed with the sensory scientists of the Sensory Analysis Center at Kansas State University,
together with professional tasters and the coffee industry.

**What it contains.** 110 attributes covering flavour, aroma and texture. For each attribute the lexicon gives a
definition and a *reference*: a specific, obtainable product or preparation that shows what the attribute tastes
like, and at what intensity. That turns tasting words into something measurable and comparable between tasters.

**Lexicon 2.0.** A revision published in October 2017 added new, globally available references for 24 attributes —
from basic tastes such as sour, bitter and salty to fruits, acids, spices and floral notes — so the references can be
sourced outside the United States.

**Link to the flavour wheel.** The lexicon is the scientific basis of the revised Coffee Taster's Flavor Wheel that
the Specialty Coffee Association and WCR published in 2016 — the first redesign of that reference poster in about
20 years.

**Access.** The lexicon can be downloaded and printed free for personal use, in English, Japanese and Chinese.
