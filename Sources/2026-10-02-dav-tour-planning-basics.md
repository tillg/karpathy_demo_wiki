# DAV: "Das ist … Tourenplanung" (tour planning explained)

*Source: https://www.alpenverein.de/artikel/das-ist-tourenplanung_82118fe9-ae5f-4fec-9dcf-36e8241cac25 (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

A DAV explainer article (May 2023) on how to plan a mountain tour, aimed at all mountain sports.

**The 3x3 framework.** Three factors are looked at again and again: the conditions (weather, state of the ground or
snow), the tour and terrain (difficulty, distance, height gain) and the people (fitness, experience, ability). They
are checked in three phases: first at home or at the hut while planning, then at checkpoints on the tour (trailhead,
key passages, summit, breaks), and finally whenever something unexpected happens (weather change, lost route, delay).

**Weather.** Use several mountain-specific forecasts (the article names the DAV's mountain weather page and the
Austrian and Swiss weather services) and the rain radar. As a rule of thumb it gets roughly 0.6 °C colder per 100 m of
altitude.

**Terrain.** Find the hard passages and how many there are, how exposed and how high the route is; dense contour lines
on the map mean steep ground. Ask hut keepers and check user platforms for current conditions.

**Time and alternatives.** Work out a realistic total time, including about 15 minutes of rest every two hours and half
an hour on top. Set checkpoints with target times and decide on alternatives before leaving.

**Group.** Plan for the weakest member; bigger groups need more buffer and honest talk about what everyone can do.

**Tools.** Guidebooks and topographic maps, online platforms such as alpenvereinaktiv.com, gipfelbuch.ch and
hikr.org, and the DAV walking-time calculator. A "risk box" lets the group mark each factor green (comfortable) or
red (at the limit); the tour goes ahead only if the overall picture stays green.

**Afterwards.** Talk through the decisions made in each phase so the group learns for next time.
