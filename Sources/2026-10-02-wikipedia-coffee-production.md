# Coffee production (Wikipedia)

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_production (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

Coffee is grown in roughly 70 countries, almost all of them in the tropics. The two commercial species are spread
unevenly across the growing regions: Latin America grows most of the world's Arabica, Asia grows most of its
Robusta, and Africa grows both in roughly equal amounts.

**Who grows the most.** Brazil is the largest Arabica producer and dries about nine tenths of its Arabica with the
dry (natural) method. Vietnam leads Robusta production. Colombia is the second-largest Arabica grower, followed by
Ethiopia and Honduras; Indonesia, India and Uganda grow both species.

**Arabica's share is shrinking.** Arabica makes up a little under 60 percent of world output today, against about
three quarters in the mid-1960s.

**Harvests.** Most producing countries have one main harvest a year. Colombia is an exception: two flowerings give a
main crop (roughly October to December) and a smaller second crop (roughly April to June).

**History.** The coffee that first reached Europe was shipped from the port of Mocha in today's Yemen; Ethiopia and
Yemen are the historical starting points of the coffee trade.

**People.** Coffee is a main source of income for about 12.5 million households, most of them in developing
countries, and around 100 million people work in the sector at least seasonally.
