# Topology: who's who and where it started

*Own notes, compiled from (accessed 2026-10-02): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Bridges_of_K%C3%B6nigsberg,
MacTutor biographies https://mathshistory.st-andrews.ac.uk/Biographies/Euler/, …/Riemann/, …/Poincare/,
…/Hausdorff/, …/Brouwer/, …/Noether_Emmy/, …/Perelman/, and https://ncatlab.org/nlab/show/Emmy+Noether.
Summarised in own words.*

**Where it started: the bridges of Königsberg.** Königsberg (today Kaliningrad) lay on both banks of the Pregel with
two islands, joined by seven bridges. Can you walk through town crossing every bridge exactly once? Euler showed in a
paper presented to the St Petersburg Academy on 26 August 1735 (published 1741) that you can't. Each time you walk
through a land mass you use one bridge in and one out, so every land mass except the start and end needs an even
number of bridges. All four had an odd number (5, 3, 3, 3). What mattered was only *which* bridges join *which*
land — not lengths or positions. That is the seed of graph theory and of topology.

**People.**

- **Leonhard Euler** (15 Apr 1707 Basel – 18 Sep 1783 St Petersburg): Königsberg bridges, the polyhedron formula,
  i.e. the Euler characteristic.
- **Bernhard Riemann** (17 Sep 1826 Breselenz – 20 Jul 1866 Selasca): Riemann surfaces (thesis 1851) and the
  10 June 1854 habilitation lecture "On the hypotheses which lie at the foundations of geometry", where manifolds
  appear.
- **Henri Poincaré** (29 Apr 1854 Nancy – 17 Jul 1912 Paris): *Analysis situs* (1895) and its sequels; fundamental
  group (MacTutor: already in his 1894 paper); the conjecture named after him. For about 40 years almost all of algebraic topology built on his work.
- **Felix Hausdorff** (8 Nov 1868 Breslau – 26 Jan 1942 Bonn): *Grundzüge der Mengenlehre* (1914), topological and
  metric spaces; Hausdorff dimension (1919). Took his own life with his wife and her sister to escape internment by
  the Nazis.
- **L. E. J. Brouwer** (27 Feb 1881 Overschie – 2 Dec 1966 Blaricum): fixed-point theorem, invariance of dimension,
  most topology done 1909–1913; later founded intuitionism.
- **Emmy Noether** (23 Mar 1882 Erlangen – 14 Apr 1935 Bryn Mawr): founder of modern abstract algebra. For topology:
  around 1925 she argued that Betti numbers and torsion coefficients should be replaced by *homology groups* —
  algebra instead of mere numbers. Dismissed by the Nazis in 1933.
- **Grigori Perelman** (born 13 Jun 1966 Leningrad): proved the Poincaré conjecture (arXiv, Nov 2002 – Jul 2003);
  declined the Fields Medal (2006) and the Millennium Prize (2010).
