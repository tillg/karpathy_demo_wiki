# Coffee production in Ethiopia (Wikipedia)

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_production_in_Ethiopia (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

Ethiopia is where Arabica comes from. The south-western Kaffa region is described as the birthplace of the Arabica
plant; wild coffee still grows there in deep forest at about 1,400–2,100 m. The well-known legend of the goat herder
Kaldi, who noticed his goats getting lively after eating the berries, is set in the 9th century but was only written
down in 1671.

**Production.** Ethiopia is Africa's largest coffee producer and around the fifth largest in the world. Coffee comes
from three kinds of farms: forest coffee from wild trees, garden coffee planted around homesteads, and plantations.
Almost all of the work is done by hand.

**Regions.** The main names on bags — Sidama (Sidamo), Yirgacheffe, Harar, Limu and Guji — are trademarked regional
designations owned by Ethiopia.

- *Sidama* grows at roughly 1,500–2,200 m; balanced cups with berry and citrus notes, lemony and bright acidity.
- *Yirgacheffe* and *Guji* are treated as very high-quality coffees within the wider Sidama area.
- *Harar*, in the eastern highlands, is one of the oldest coffees still produced; it is known for a fruity,
  wine-like character and is processed almost entirely by hand.

Because the wild Arabica forests are here, plant collectors have gathered coffee in Ethiopia for a long time —
Geisha is one of the varieties that started out as such a collection.
