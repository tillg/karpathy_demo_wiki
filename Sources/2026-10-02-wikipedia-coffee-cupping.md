# Coffee cupping (Wikipedia)

*Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_cupping (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

Cupping is the standard way coffee professionals taste and compare coffees side by side.

**Procedure.** Freshly roasted, freshly ground coffee is steeped with water heated to just below boiling.
The taster first sniffs the coffee deeply, then takes small spoonfuls (about 4–5 ml) and slurps them loudly. Slurping sprays the coffee across the tongue and palate and pulls air through it, so
aroma and taste are picked up at the same time.

**What is assessed:**

- *Aroma:* described with families such as fruity, floral, nutty, chocolate-like, earthy and spicy.
- *Taste:* acidity, bitterness, sweetness and sourness.
- *Mouthfeel:* body (from thin to full) and astringency, the drying sensation after swallowing.

Experienced cuppers try to recognise a coffee's origin from the flavour patterns typical of a region.

**Who does it.** Certified professional tasters are called Q Graders; the Specialty Coffee Association publishes the
forms and handbooks used for formal evaluation. The article itself does not explain the scoring scale in detail.
