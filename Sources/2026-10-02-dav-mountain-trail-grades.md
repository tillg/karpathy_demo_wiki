# DAV: "Schwierigkeitsgrade von Bergwegen" (difficulty grades of mountain trails)

*Source: https://www.alpenverein.de/artikel/schwierigkeitsgrade-von-bergwegen_8de95a6d-b490-4963-86cf-a0907c48a331 (accessed 2026-10-02). Summarised in own words.*

A DAV page explaining how mountain trails in the Alps are graded and signed.

**Colour on the signposts.** Yellow signposts carry a colour for the difficulty: blue = easy, red = medium, black =
difficult. The paint marks on trees and rocks along the way are usually red-white-red whatever the grade.

**DAV categories and the SAC scale.**

- *Easy mountain trail (blue, about T2):* mostly narrow, can be steep, no places with a risk of falling.
- *Medium mountain trail (red, about T3):* narrow and steep, may have exposed sections and short passages secured with
  cables or chains.
- *Difficult mountain trail (black, T4–T6):* steep, exposed, with places where hands are needed and simple climbing;
  needs sure-footedness and a head for heights.

The Swiss Alpine Club (SAC) hiking scale has six grades. T1 is a simple valley path; T1–T2 is ordinary hiking with
little exposure; at T3–T4 alpine experience matters more and hands are used for balance; T5–T6 are often pathless,
need climbing skill (up to grade II at T6) and familiarity with ice axe and rope.

**Caveat.** The grades assume normal, good conditions. Wet rock, rain or old snow can make a route much harder.
