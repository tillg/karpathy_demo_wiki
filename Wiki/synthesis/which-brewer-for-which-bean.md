# Which brewer for which bean?

An analysis across the vault's pages — a rule of thumb, not a law. The sources describe brewers and beans separately;
the pairing below is the vault's own inference from them.

**Two axes decide it.**

1. *Filter → clarity vs body.* Thick paper ([[chemex]]) holds back most oils: cleanest cup. Thinner paper ([[v60]])
   is a bit fuller. A mesh ([[french-press]]) lets the oils through: most body.
2. *Contact → immersion vs percolation vs pressure.* Immersion ([[french-press]], partly [[aeropress]]) steeps all
   grounds evenly and is forgiving; pour-over gives control through grind and pouring; pressure
   ([[espresso-machine]], [[moka-pot]]) concentrates everything — including roast bitterness.

**Pairings.**

| Bean | Why | Brewer |
|---|---|---|
| Light-roast [[geisha]], washed [[coffee-ethiopia]] | delicate florals, citrus, high acidity — easily hidden by oils and roast | [[chemex]], [[v60]] |
| Washed [[coffee-kenya]] | clean, bright cup from washing and soaking | [[v60]] |
| Natural or honey coffees, e.g. [[coffee-brazil]] | sweetness and body from the fruit left on during drying | [[french-press]], [[aeropress]] |
| Medium to dark roasts | origin already masked, roast flavours and body to the fore | [[espresso-machine]], [[moka-pot]], [[french-press]] |

**Pour-over vs immersion in one line:** pour-over (~3–4 min, medium to medium-fine) rewards a precise hand with
clarity; immersion (~4 min, coarse) forgives mistakes and rewards with body — but pour it off the grounds in time.

Whatever the brewer, the same levers apply: [[brew-ratio]], [[grind-size]], [[extraction]], [[water-for-coffee]].
Testing: tasting notes now go in the [[brew-log]]. The first entry (a natural Ethiopian on the [[v60]]) was
under-extracted, so the naturals → immersion pairing above is still untested.
