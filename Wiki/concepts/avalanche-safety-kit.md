# Avalanche safety kit

> [!warning] Example data
> This page summarises two sources for the demo. It is not safety advice: take an avalanche course and check the
> current bulletin before every tour.

The DAV's 10 ski-touring recommendations list the standard emergency kit for every ski tour: **avalanche transceiver,
probe and shovel**, plus first-aid kit, bivvy bag and mobile phone. An **airbag** raises the chance of survival.
Check the kit before starting and carry a repair kit. The own course notes agree: transceiver,
shovel, probe — always.

On glaciers add harness, rope and crevasse rescue kit ([[glacier-travel]]). The DAV points to training and avalanche
courses for the practical basics; the phone matters for the [[alpine-emergency]] call.

Related behaviour from the same leaflet: about 10 m spacing on steep ascents, at least 30 m on descent, very steep
slopes one at a time, groups of up to six.

## Checklist

Before leaving the car park:

- [x] Transceiver on, in send mode, batteries checked
- [x] Probe and shovel in the pack
- [x] First-aid kit and bivvy bag
- [x] Phone charged; emergency number 112 saved
- [ ] Airbag armed (if carried)
- [ ] Group transceiver check done
- [ ] Glacier: harness, rope, crevasse rescue kit ([[glacier-travel]])
