# Knot

**Intuition:** a tangled string with its ends glued together, so it can't be untied without cutting.

**Definition:** an embedding of a circle in three-dimensional space. Two knots are equal if one can be moved into the
other by an *ambient isotopy* — deforming the surrounding space without the string passing through itself.

**Examples:** the *unknot* (a plain ring) and the *trefoil*, the simplest true knot.

**Telling knots apart:** diagrams of the same knot are linked by the three Reidemeister moves (1927). Invariants:
crossing number, Alexander polynomial (1928), Jones polynomial (1984). Every knot on its own is
[[homeomorphism|homeomorphic]] to a circle — what differs is how it sits in space. See
[[topological-invariants-overview]].

Applications: DNA and enzymes, molecular chirality, topological quantum computing.
