# Compactness

**Intuition:** a space that is "finite in spirit": you can't escape to infinity or sneak up on a missing edge point.

**Definition:** every cover by [[open-set]]s has a finite subcover.

**In Euclidean space (Heine–Borel):** compact = closed and bounded. So [0, 1] is compact, (0, 1) and the real line
are not.

**Why it pays:** a continuous real function on a nonempty compact space reaches a maximum and a minimum; continuous
images of compact spaces are compact. It is a hypothesis of the [[brouwer-fixed-point-theorem]] and of
"closed" surfaces in the [[classification-of-surfaces]].

Term from Fréchet (1906); open-cover form from Alexandrov and Urysohn. See [[topological-invariants-overview]].
