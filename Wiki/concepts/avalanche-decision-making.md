# Avalanche decision-making

How the DAV describes reaching decisions in avalanche terrain (educational summary, not advice for a specific tour —
take a course and read the daily bulletin):

- **Risk = hazard × consequences − measures** (German "G × K − M = R"). Risk can't be measured exactly.
- **Avalanche mantra:** probabilistic tools → local snow and terrain analysis → consequences → measures → human factor.
- **Probabilistic / reduction methods** such as the SnowCard and skitourenguru.ch match terrain to the danger level
  ([[avalanche-danger-scale]]). Slopes steeper than about **30°** count as avalanche terrain.
- **Warning signs:** fresh snow, wind slab, shooting cracks, "whumpf" sounds, recent avalanches.
- **Human factor:** group pressure, biases and poor communication — name them openly.
- When in doubt, avoid the slope or turn back (DAV recommendation 5).

Fits into the "single slope" phase of [[tour-planning-3x3]]. Slope [[aspect]] links the bulletin's danger spots to
the terrain; in spring the wet-snow problem is time-dependent ([[spring-snow]]).
